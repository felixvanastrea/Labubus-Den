## Acute rheumatic fever

30 past questions. Two ideas make up almost half of them: the major Jones criteria and the basic facts (age, delay, germ).

**Major Jones criteria (×8, in every kind of exam)**

| Major (5) | Not major: the traps |
| --- | --- |
| Carditis | Arthralgia or polyarthralgia (minor): the trap in nearly every version |
| Migratory polyarthritis | Raised ESR / CRP (minor) |
| Sydenham's chorea | Previous rheumatic fever (minor) |
| Erythema marginatum | Mitral stenosis, mitral regurgitation (not criteria) |
| Subcutaneous nodules | Convulsions, skin abscess (nothing to do with it) |

- Arthritis is major, arthralgia is minor. If the option says "arthralgia", it is not major.
- "All of the above" is false when polyarthralgia is in the list.

**Basic facts (×5)**

- Age: 5 to 15 years (5 to 14).
- Germ: group A beta-hemolytic streptococcus (Streptococcus pyogenes), after a throat infection. Not pneumococcus, not staph.
- Delay after the pharyngitis: weeks, never days. Keyed answers: "2 to 4 weeks", "1 to 3 weeks". "2 to 4 days" is always false.
- Mechanism: antigen mimicry and a delayed autoimmune reaction. Not spread of infection or of inflammation. Pericarditis is not direct bacterial invasion.
- Most affected valve: mitral.
- "The brain is definitely involved": false. "Recurrence is prevented by corticosteroids": false.

**Diagnosis (×2, plus one on the lab test)**

- Proof of a strep throat infection + 2 major criteria (or 1 major + 2 minor).
- False: 2 major alone, 1 major + 1 minor, 2 minor.
- Most important lab test: ASO titre. Not blood culture, not blood count.
- Best imaging for early rheumatic heart disease: echocardiography.

**The arthritis (×2)**

- Migratory polyarthritis of the large joints; each joint lasts a few days to a week.
- Never chronic, persistent, symmetrical, single-joint or small-joint. Not always with nodules.

**Skin and chorea**

- Erythema marginatum: not itchy, transient. Nodules: painless, over extensor surfaces. Skin signs are rare: 5 to 10%.
- Chorea: rapid, involuntary, non-rhythmic movements; a major criterion; children, not the elderly; comes late, not 5 days after tonsillitis.

**Complications (×3)**

- During the attack: pancarditis, so mitral regurgitation and pericarditis.
- Not: aortic stenosis (that comes years later), myocardial infarction, stroke, chronic polyarthritis, skin infection.

**Treatment and prevention (×2)**

- Inflammation: aspirin / NSAIDs; if not tolerated, corticosteroids.
- Primary prevention: cure the first strep throat.
- Secondary prophylaxis: prevents recurrences. Benzathine penicillin G every 2 to 4 weeks, not weekly. It is not surgery and does not prevent the first episode.

## Mitral stenosis

21 past questions, 5 of them in last year's midterm.

- **Auscultation (×6):** opening snap, then a diastolic rumble at the apex. Loud S1. Diastolic thrill at the apex. Wrong: holosystolic or pansystolic (that's MR), harsh systolic, continuous, ejection click, upper left sternal border.
- **Consequences (×4):** left atrium dilated, pulmonary hypertension, right ventricular dysfunction. Wrong: left ventricle enlarged, arterial (systemic) hypertension, "chest pain is the common symptom".
- **Symptoms of severe MS (×3):** hemoptysis, palpitations, orthopnea, dysphagia (big left atrium on the esophagus), dysphonia. Not syncope, not angina, not headaches.
- **Cause (×3):** rheumatic heart disease. Not prolapse, not chordal rupture (those give regurgitation), not endocarditis, not degenerative, not congenital.
- **Complications (×2):** atrial fibrillation (late), stroke, right heart failure. Not pulmonary embolism, not left ventricular dysfunction, not aortic dissection.
- **Numbers:** normal area 4 to 6 cm² ("2 to 4" is false). Severe under 1.5 cm².
- **Treatment (×2):** balloon valvotomy when the valve is tight (1.5 cm² or less) and its morphology favourable. Contraindicated with a left atrial thrombus or moderate mitral regurgitation; not for mild MS before pregnancy. Unfavourable anatomy: surgical valve replacement.

## Mitral regurgitation

15 past questions, 11 of them on two ideas. None in last year's midterm.

- **Murmur (×7):** holosystolic (pansystolic) at the apex, radiating to the left axilla. Wrong: diastolic at the mitral area (MS), right axilla, upper sternal border, ejection murmur, continuous, pulsus parvus et tardus, jugular distension.
- The same question often adds: it can be caused by chordal rupture after a myocardial infarction (true), and "cannot be caused by mitral valve prolapse" (false).
- **Primary etiologies (×4):** mitral valve prolapse and rheumatic disease. Not ischemic heart disease, not atrial fibrillation (those are secondary). Most common cause in low-income countries: rheumatic.
- **Mitral apparatus:** annulus, leaflets, chordae, left ventricular papillary muscles (pillars). Not the outflow tract, not right ventricular chordae.
- **Imaging:** transthoracic echo and transesophageal echo. Not X-ray, ECG or MRI.
- **Complications:** endocarditis, heart failure, atrial fibrillation, ventricular arrhythmias. Not pulmonary embolism.
- **Treatment of severe symptomatic primary MR:** valve repair, or replacement. Not medical therapy alone, not TAVI, not anticoagulation.

## Aortic stenosis

26 past questions, 5 in last year's midterm. The "which statements are true" question came 8 times with the same handful of statements shuffled.

**The recurring statements (×8)**

| True | False |
| --- | --- |
| Crescendo-decrescendo ejection murmur at the upper sternal border is the hallmark | Angina in AS is always due to coronary artery disease |
| Echocardiography is the best tool for severity | It can be secondary to infective endocarditis, or to aortic dissection |
| Symptomatic severe AS can lead to sudden death | Left ventricular enlargement is the initial mechanism (it is hypertrophy) |
| Primary treatment of symptomatic and severe AS is valve replacement | Severe AS can be treated by medical treatment only |
| Moderate: area 1 to 1.5 cm², jet 3 to 4 m/s | Severe: area 1 to 1.5 cm² (severe is under 1) |
| Rheumatic fever is an etiology in adults | Rheumatic fever is the most common cause in developed countries |
| Coronary angiography before surgery in adults over 50 | The murmur is diastolic; chest X-ray is the reliable test |

- Watch the wording: "severe AS must be treated only by surgery or TAVI" is keyed false (two versions), and so is the same sentence with "moderate". The true version always says "symptomatic and severe".

**Triad (×6):** dyspnea, angina, syncope or lipothymia. Not palpitations, cough, hemoptysis, nausea.

**Complications (×4):** sudden cardiac death, ventricular arrhythmias, right ventricular dysfunction (late). Never pulmonary embolism or aortic dissection.

**Treatment (×3):** aortic valve replacement, surgical or TAVI. Not diuretics, beta-blockers, annuloplasty, mitral commissurotomy.

**Exam and tests:** systolic thrill at the upper sternum, soft or absent A2. Not bounding pulses, not dancing carotids (AR). ECG: left ventricular hypertrophy. Gold standard: echocardiography. Elderly in developed countries: calcific degeneration. Consequences: left ventricular hypertrophy and left atrial dilation.

## Aortic regurgitation

9 past questions, none repeated and none in last year's midterm: the lowest priority of the eight.

- **Murmur:** high-pitched, blowing, diastolic, along the left sternal border.
- **Peripheral signs:** bounding pulse, wide pulse pressure, head bobbing, dancing carotids. Not narrow pulse pressure, weak pulses, edema or hepatojugular reflux.
- **Acute AR:** infective endocarditis. Chronic: rheumatic, bicuspid valve, aortic aneurysm; it can be secondary to hypertension.
- **Consequence:** eccentric hypertrophy and left ventricular enlargement.
- **First-line test:** Doppler echocardiography.
- **Complications:** left ventricular dysfunction, ventricular arrhythmias, infective endocarditis.

## Infective endocarditis

18 past questions, 5 in last year's midterm.

- **Highest-risk cardiopathy (×4):** a prosthetic valve, mechanical or bioprosthesis. Not mitral stenosis (intermediate), not atrial septal defect (the one congenital lesion not at risk), not a coronary bypass graft.
- **When to suspect it (×4):** fever + a murmur (an aortic insufficiency murmur counts), fever + heart failure, fever + hemiplegia in a young patient. Not fever + sore throat or tonsillitis, fever + dyspnea, fever + chest pain, angina.
- **Duke major criteria (×2):** positive blood cultures for typical germs; echo lesions: vegetation, perforation, abscess. Minor: Janeway lesions, embolic events such as a brain abscess. Definite IE = 2 major, or 1 major + 3 minor, or 5 minor (1 major + 2 minor is false).
- **Right-sided IE (×2):** IV drug users, septic pulmonary emboli and lung abscesses, Staphylococcus aureus. Less common than left-sided. Not streptococci, not coronary emboli.
- **Germs overall:** streptococci (viridans) are still more common than staphylococci.
- **Where it grafts:** valve leaflets, prosthetic valves, pacemakers. Not pulmonary veins.
- **Triggers:** tooth extraction, infected skin wound, purulent otitis. Not a digestive endoscopy.
- **Signs:** fever and poor general condition, splenomegaly, Janeway lesions, purpura.
- **Consequences and complications:** valve tears and perforation, perivalvular abscess, heart failure, ischemic stroke, renal infarction, splenic abscess, mycotic aneurysm. Not DVT, not atherosclerotic aneurysm.
- **Treatment:** blood cultures before antibiotics; IV, bactericidal, combined antibiotics for 4 to 6 weeks, never oral, never a single drug "because it works in vitro". Acute AR with shock: urgent valve replacement after stabilisation. Prophylaxis before dental care in prosthetic valve holders.

## Acute pericarditis

9 past questions on four ideas, each asked twice in different words. None in last year's midterm.

- **Pathophysiology:** both layers (parietal and visceral) inflamed, and the myocardium can be involved. Not the parietal layer alone, not the endocardium, not pancarditis.
- **Most common cause:** viral (or idiopathic). Not bacterial, fungal or parasitic.
- **In favour:** sharp pleuritic chest pain worse lying down; pericardial friction rub. Not Q waves on the ECG (infarction), not pericardial calcification (chronic constriction).
- **Complications:** tamponade, constriction, recurrence with effusion, supraventricular arrhythmias. Not aortic dissection, not pericardial rupture, not myocardial infarction.

## Hypertension

19 past questions, mostly single ones from the in-class lists. None in last year's midterm.

- **Emergency vs urgency (×4):** both 180 or more. Emergency = with target organ damage, in hospital (hypertensive encephalopathy is one). Urgency = no organ damage, no hospitalisation.
- **Secondary causes (×3):** coarctation, thyroid disease, Conn's adenoma, acute glomerulonephritis, pregnancy. Not congenital aortic stenosis, not atherosclerosis.
- **Definition (×2):** office 140/90 or more ("over 139/89"). 135/85 is the home threshold; 130/80 is the treatment target.
- **Goals:** first under 140/80, then under 130/80 if tolerated.
- **Measuring:** home BP and ambulatory BP are the reliable methods; ABPM confirms hypertension, finds masked hypertension, rules out white coat, detects orthostatic changes. Masked = high at home, normal at the office. Sustained = high in both.
- **Tests:** lipids, glucose, creatinine, urine albumin/creatinine ratio, potassium, blood count. Not TSH routinely.
- **Asked as yes/no:** the leading cardiovascular risk factor for death (yes); uncontrolled in more than 50% worldwide (yes).
- **Complications:** stroke, kidney failure. Not aortic stenosis, not pulmonary embolism.
- **Drugs:** first line: ACE inhibitors (or ARBs), calcium channel blockers, thiazides. Not central drugs. Diabetic with coronary disease: beta-blockers and ACE inhibitors/ARBs.
- **Side effects:** ACE inhibitors: dry cough. Calcium blockers: ankle edema. Beta-blockers: erectile dysfunction. Edema is not from diuretics; dehydration is not from ARBs.
- **Lifestyle:** weight loss, stop smoking, exercise. Not rest, not unlimited alcohol.
